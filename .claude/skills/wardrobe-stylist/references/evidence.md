# Evidence and editorial references

Reviewed October 11, 2026. These sources support a cautious styling method, not a validated algorithm for female attraction. Routine advice should still be concrete and useful.

## User-provided style reference

[Samantha (@alecttrona), “How to look ridiculously handsome with little effort”, February 3, 2026](<https://x.com/alecttrona/status/2018557349775208791>) is an editorial X article. It advocates simple contrasting combinations: black/white, olive/white, brown/cream and blue/charcoal, including dark outerwear and trousers with a light shirt. Its collages are inspiration, not evidence of the user's ownership or actual fit.

**Use:** consider a simple contrast-led candidate when appropriate. Do not turn it into “always use maximum contrast,” an attraction guarantee, or a scientific claim about women. The user shared the reference but did not say every recommendation must follow it.

## Colour coordination: primary experiment

[Gray, Schmitt, Strohminger & Kassam (2014), *The Science of Style: In Fashion, Colors Should Match Only Moderately*, PLOS ONE](<https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0102772>).

239 online participants, 69% women, evaluated colour combinations in men's and women's outfits. The reported relationship favoured moderate matching rather than the most matched or most clashing combinations.

**Use:** consider balance as well as contrast. **Limit:** the outcome was judged fashionableness, not real-world romantic success, not a study specifically of 22-year-old women's preferences, and not evidence that every tonal or high-contrast outfit is worse. Colour coordination and light/dark contrast are not identical constructs. This is not a head-to-head test of the X article.

## Clothing detail and first impressions

[Howlett et al. (2013), University of Hertfordshire publication record](<https://researchprofiles.herts.ac.uk/en/publications/the-influence-of-clothing-on-first-impressions-rapid-and-positive/>), DOI 10.1108/13612021311305128. [Author's earlier study summary](<https://karenpine.com/wp-content/uploads/2011/09/Executive-summary_The-Effect-of-Appearance-on-First-Impressions.pdf>) describes 306 participants, brief viewing of face-obscured models, and more favourable ratings on several attributes for the male model in a bespoke versus off-the-peg suit.

**Use:** small garment differences can be worth checking. **Limit:** formal suits, restricted stimuli, first-impression attributes rather than dating outcomes, and industry collaboration in the earlier summary. It does not establish that expensive clothing, tighter clothes or suits are best for a casual social occasion. Do not extrapolate exact tailoring advice from a user's height and weight.

## Broader context: review, not a new experiment

[Hester & Hehman (2023), *Dress is a Fundamental Component of Person Perception*](<https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10559650/>), DOI 10.1177/10888683231157961.

The review treats dress, face, body, situation and the observer's cultural knowledge as interacting contributors to impressions. It describes methodological and contextual limitations in the research.

**Use:** adapt to audience, setting and the user's feedback rather than assert one universal female gaze. **Limit:** a conceptual review cannot predict an individual's attraction or justify a numerical outfit attractiveness score.

## Decision rule

Use the studies to constrain overclaims, not decorate an opinion with scientific language. Label practical choices as stylistic judgments. Learn from the user's own likes, comfort and real feedback, without assuming an outfit caused a social outcome. No percentage uplift, body-ratio target, dating guarantee, or medical claim about lifts is supported by this evidence set.
